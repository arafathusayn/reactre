# What still differs between @reactre/compat and React 19.3.1

An audit of every difference documented in `packages/compat/README.md` ("Where the client differs" and the server's list), in `core.ts`'s head comment and in comments in `packages/compat/src`, and of those found while checking them against React's production builds (`react@19.3.1`, `react-dom@19.3.1`) on the fake DOM. Each is classed by what matching React would cost:

- **(a)** nothing on the normal path: the work runs only on an error, a suspension, a transition, hydration or another rare path, or in a feature module;
- **(b)** a measurable cost on the normal path (the hot render functions, or every hook or host update);
- **(c)** not possible, or not without giving up what compat is (it writes the DOM as it renders, with one fiber tree).

Measured A/Bs show that even a small branch in a hot function (runHost, handlersOn, reconcile, runFunc's normal path, place, create) costs 2 to 4% on the JS framework benchmark, so (b) items stay as they are.

## How each closed item is checked

Every closed item has a test that runs the same scene on React's production client and on compat and expects the same log (renders, effects, refs, errors with their component stacks, markup), and that failed before the change:

- 1, 4, 5: `client.test.ts`, "insertion effects, layout cleanups, ref detaches and componentWillUnmount wait for the whole render" (with cleanups that throw, caught and uncaught).
- 2, 3: `client.test.ts`, "an update's invalid props throw as it commits" (six kinds, caught and uncaught).
- 6: `client.test.ts`, "dangerouslySetInnerHTML not in the form {\_\_html} (#61), or with children (#60)".
- 7: `client.test.ts`, "a component whose first hook is use() of a promise has its frame"; `server.test.ts`, the stack of a fallback (S7).
- 8: `client.test.ts`, "an update rendered again calls getSnapshotBeforeUpdate once".
- 10, 11, 12: `concurrent.test.ts`, whose scenes now compare renders too (one excepted, see 31), and "a transition whose updates leave state as it was renders the component, and not its children".
- 13, 14: `concurrent.test.ts`, "a transition undone puts back the document metadata it hoisted to <head>" and "... the form fields' values and checkedness".
- S3: `server.test.ts`, "a component that sets state while it renders takes each pass's action and initial state".
- 18: `concurrent.test.ts`, "throttled reveals" (a reveal held until the throttle ends, an update that drops it, a second reveal that joins it, and the text, attributes, nodes and fields a held reveal makes again).
- 19: `client.test.ts`, "an update that reaches a dehydrated boundary hydrates it first, with what it had, then renders", whose shells now log their renders.
- 20: `viewTransitions.test.ts` (eight scenes on fake `startViewTransition`, layout and animations: updates, enters, exits and pairs, types and classes by type, `none`, out of view, inline and clipped elements, Activity, keyed moves, bail-outs, compiled templates, reveals, no API, a skipped transition, a forced commit and a commit that waits), and the headless-Chrome scenario "view transitions: names, types and events" in `apps/react-compat` (compat and compat with the compiler; core alone has no module for it).
- 16: `concurrent.test.ts`, "a transition undone puts back its selects' selection" (with a select and options the test makes for both libraries), and the headless-Chrome scenario "a transition undone keeps a select's selection" in `apps/react-compat`.
